\subsubsection{Metadata artefact control}
\label{sec:artefact}
A second contamination is not about which images recur but about how they were
produced. In the AdversarialEdit split every one of the 1{,}000 manipulated
images is $1408 \times 768$, while the authentic images are spread over many
resolutions, formats and colour modes; AdversarialGen carries the same
signature in 995 of its 1{,}000 manipulated images. The classes are therefore
separable without looking at a single pixel. We confirm this directly: a
classifier given only width, height, area, aspect ratio, file size, format and
colour mode---no image content whatsoever---reaches
$100.00 \pm 0.00$ percent accuracy under the grouped protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:training}. Any model with access to the file as stored can
score perfectly here for the wrong reason, and a headline accuracy near
95 percent means nothing until that is ruled out.

We therefore re-encode every image through one identical pipeline before
feature extraction: resized to $384 \times 384$, converted to RGB, saved as
JPEG at quality 90, with EXIF discarded. Resolution, aspect ratio, format,
colour mode and EXIF are destroyed; content is preserved. Repeating the
CLIP-only configuration on the re-encoded corpus, with the same folds,
hyperparameters and cut-off, gives $94.35 \pm 0.94$ percent against
$95.38 \pm 1.33$ on the originals---a loss of $1.03$ points. The discriminative
signal is in the pixels, not in the provenance fingerprint.

Two limits on what this establishes. Re-encoding removes the \emph{storage}
artefact, not a generation-pipeline signature that survives resampling: if
every manipulated image carries the same synthesis fingerprint, a visual model
may be recognising the generator rather than the manipulation, and the
construction-by-pipeline labelling of Section~\ref{sec:data} makes that
difficult to separate even in principle. And the distribution-shift evaluation
of Section~\ref{sec:shift} is run on the original files, so its figures carry
the uncontrolled artefact; re-running that protocol on re-encoded images is the
control we have not done. We report the same-distribution number both ways and
flag the shift number as uncontrolled rather than quietly reporting the larger
of the two.
